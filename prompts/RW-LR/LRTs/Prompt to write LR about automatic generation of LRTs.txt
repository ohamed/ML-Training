Act as a researcher specializing in Natural Language Processing, language assessment, psycholinguistics, and computer-assisted language learning.

I am preparing a research paper on the automatic generation of Lexical Recognition Tests (LRTs) using Natural Language Processing and Large Language Models.

I will upload a set of my research papers published between 2015 and 2019. These papers focus on LRT generation using NLP techniques and should serve as the historical foundation for the literature review.

Your task is to write a comprehensive, critical, and chronologically structured literature review on the generation of Lexical Recognition Tests and related lexical vocabulary assessment instruments.

Use the uploaded papers as primary sources for the period 2015–2019. Then extend the literature review by identifying relevant peer-reviewed research published from 2020 through 2026.

Research scope

Focus on research related to:

- Lexical Recognition Tests and their development
- Automatic or semi-automatic generation of LRTs
- Vocabulary assessment and lexical knowledge testing
- NLP-based generation of vocabulary assessment items
- Computational approaches to selecting words and nonwords for lexical tests
- Automatic generation of lexical decision tasks
- Computational psycholinguistics approaches relevant to LRT construction
- Word frequency, lexical frequency, familiarity, length, morphology, and other lexical properties used for item selection
- Distractor and nonword generation
- Validation and quality control of automatically generated test items
- Human evaluation of generated lexical test items
- Machine learning approaches for lexical assessment
- Transformer-based approaches to lexical assessment
- Large Language Models and generative AI for language-test or vocabulary-item generation
- Recent work using LLMs to generate, evaluate, or validate language assessment items

Important distinction

Keep the focus on LRT generation rather than general language assessment.

Do not fill the review with studies that only discuss vocabulary learning, vocabulary acquisition, language proficiency, or general NLP unless they provide a clear methodological connection to LRT construction or automatic lexical test generation.

Literature review structure

Organize the review into the following sections.

1. Introduction to LRTs and lexical recognition assessment

Explain what an LRT measures and how it differs from other vocabulary and language assessment instruments.

Discuss the importance of word and nonword selection.

2. Early computational approaches to LRT generation

Review the 2015–2019 uploaded studies.

Explain how NLP was used to automate or support LRT construction.

Compare the approaches rather than describing each paper independently.

3. NLP-based LRT generation

Synthesize the computational techniques used for:

- Candidate word selection
- Word filtering
- Frequency-based selection
- Linguistic annotation
- Morphological analysis
- Nonword generation
- Distractor construction
- Item validation

Identify common methodological patterns and differences between studies.

4. Developments from 2020 onward

Review how approaches changed after 2020.

Discuss the transition from traditional NLP and rule-based methods toward machine learning, neural models, transformers, and generative models.

5. LLMs and generative AI for lexical test generation

Identify relevant research involving LLMs or generative AI.

Discuss whether LLMs have been used to:

- Generate candidate words
- Generate nonwords
- Generate complete lexical tests
- Filter or rank candidate items
- Evaluate lexical items
- Validate generated test items
- Replace or complement traditional NLP pipelines

Clearly distinguish direct evidence from studies that are only indirectly relevant.

6. Validation of automatically generated LRTs

Review how researchers validate generated lexical tests.

Discuss:

- Human evaluation
- Expert judgments
- Native-speaker judgments
- Pilot testing
- Psychometric analysis
- Reliability
- Validity
- Item difficulty
- Discrimination
- Statistical testing

Highlight which validation procedures are well established and which remain underdeveloped.

7. Research gap

Identify the main limitations in existing research.

Pay particular attention to:

- Limited use of modern LLMs for LRT generation
- Lack of systematic comparison between traditional NLP and LLM-based approaches
- Lack of transparent stimulus-generation pipelines
- Limited human validation
- Limited psychometric validation
- Lack of reproducibility
- Lack of systematic evaluation of generated nonwords
- Lack of comparison between different LLMs
- Lack of studies examining whether LLM-generated LRT items preserve the properties of established LRTs

Use this section to establish a clear research gap that can motivate a new study on LLM-generated LRTs.

8. Summary and research direction

Summarize the evolution of LRT generation from traditional NLP methods to current generative AI approaches.

Explain what remains unresolved and what future research should investigate.

Critical synthesis

Do not write a paper-by-paper annotated bibliography.

Instead:

- Compare methodologies.
- Identify trends.
- Explain methodological improvements over time.
- Identify contradictions between studies.
- Discuss limitations.
- Explain why particular approaches were adopted.
- Identify unresolved problems.
- Connect older NLP approaches with current LLM-based approaches.

Create a conceptual evolution such as:

Traditional lexical resources → NLP-based generation → machine learning → neural/transformer approaches → LLM-based generation.

However, only include stages supported by the literature.
